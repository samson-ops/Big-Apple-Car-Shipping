New York to Colorado car shipping is a mid-length run with one big variable: where in Colorado the car is going. The NYC metro to Denver is roughly 1,800 miles and takes four to six days on an open carrier, with a running mid-size sedan typically pricing at $1,100 to $1,200. Boulder, Fort Collins, and Colorado Springs sit within $50 of that. Vail, Breckenridge, Aspen, Steamboat, and Telluride are a different job, and much of this page is about why.

We ship this lane for relocating tech workers, Fort Carson and Air Force families, students, and people who simply want to live near the trailheads. Pickups run daily from our Bronx, Yonkers, and Long Island offices, and from Buffalo and Albany upstate. The quote tool beside the headline prices any Colorado zip, mountain towns included.

I-80 to I-76, or I-70 from St. Louis: how the car gets there

Most carriers run I-80 west through Pennsylvania, Ohio, Indiana, and Iowa, then pick up I-76 at Big Springs, Nebraska, which drops straight into Denver from the northeast. The alternative is I-70 from St. Louis across Kansas, which carriers take when they have a Kansas City drop on the load, or when a blizzard closes I-80 across Nebraska, which happens a few times a winter. Both routes land on the Denver side of the Rockies before any mountain pass.

From Buffalo or Rochester the car joins I-90 to Chicago and then I-80, and the run is only about 1,500 miles. That trims a day and roughly $100 to $150 off the metro price for anyone booking through the Buffalo office.

Front Range delivery: Denver, Boulder, Fort Collins, Colorado Springs

Denver is an easy city for a car hauler. The streets are wide, I-25, I-70, and the 470 beltway ring the metro, and most neighborhoods take a nine-car truck. The exceptions are the tight older blocks in the Highlands and the alleys of Cherry Creek North, where drivers meet you on Speer or Colorado Boulevard. Aurora, Lakewood, Littleton, Highlands Ranch, Broomfield, and Westminster are all straightforward suburban drops.

Boulder adds about 30 miles and $25 to $50, and the CU campus area is tight enough that drivers use the Table Mesa park-and-ride. Fort Collins is similar via I-25 north. Colorado Springs, 70 miles south on I-25, is where military car shipping traffic goes: Fort Carson on the south side, Peterson Space Force Base by the airport, and the Air Force Academy north of town. All three gates require the driver to meet you outside, so plan a parking lot handoff.

Vail, Breckenridge, Aspen, Steamboat, Telluride: the mountain surcharge

Mountain delivery costs $200 to $400 above the Denver price because a loaded 80-foot hauler either cannot or should not go there. West of Denver, I-70 climbs to 11,000 feet at the Eisenhower Tunnel, and Vail Pass beyond it closes to trucks in storms. Colorado's commercial chain law applies on I-70 from September through May. Aspen is a further 40 miles off I-70 on State Route 82, Steamboat is over Rabbit Ears Pass on US-40, and Telluride is 330 miles from Denver with no interstate at all.

So most mountain cars are delivered one of two ways. Either the big truck drops the car at a yard in the Denver area and a three-car or single-car truck takes it up, which is where the surcharge and an extra one to three days come from, or you pick it up in Denver yourself. Fly-and-ship customers landing at DIA often choose the second option. Breckenridge and Keystone are the easiest mountain towns because they sit beside I-70 at Frisco; Telluride is the hardest. Expedited service covers the Denver leg, but the mountain leg runs on the small truck's schedule.

Ski-season timing and condo HOA rules

If the car is going to a ski condo, book early. December and the weeks around Presidents' Day are the tightest, because every small truck in Summit County is busy and I-70 closes for hours after heavy snow. Many condo associations in Vail, Breckenridge, and Aspen prohibit commercial vehicles in their lots, and underground garages have seven-foot clearance limits, so delivery is almost always at a surface lot or town parking structure. Ask the HOA before you book and give us the meeting spot. Summer is the quiet season and the cheapest time to move a car to a mountain town on an open carrier.

Who is moving to Colorado, and what they ship

Colorado grew fast through the 2010s on tech hiring in Boulder and Denver, the early cannabis economy, and remote workers who chose the state for the trails. The cars we see reflect it: Subaru Outbacks and Foresters, Toyota 4Runners and Tacomas, and a lot of crossovers with roof boxes and bike racks. Remove the roof box, bike rack, and any crossbars that raise the car before pickup. An open carrier's top deck sits under a 13-foot-6 height limit, and a roof box can push a tall SUV over it, which means a height surcharge or a refused load. Racks also are not covered by cargo insurance. SUVs and pickups add $100 to $250 over the sedan price.

Altitude, tire pressure, and Front Range emissions testing

Altitude is not a problem for a shipped car. Modern fuel injection adjusts to thin air on its own. The one thing to check on delivery is tire pressure, which reads a few PSI higher at 5,280 feet and higher again in the mountains; set it to the door-sticker number once the tires are cold. A weak battery should be replaced before shipping, because cold starts at altitude expose it.

Colorado requires an emissions test for registration in the Front Range counties (Denver, Boulder, Broomfield, Jefferson, and parts of Adams, Arapahoe, Larimer, and Weld) for gasoline cars more than seven model years old, and newer cars are exempt. A New York car new to the state also needs a VIN verification, which the Air Care Colorado test station can do. Mountain counties and Colorado Springs have no emissions test. Questions about New York to Colorado car shipping

How much does it cost to ship a car from New York to Denver?

A running mid-size sedan on an open carrier from the NYC metro to the Denver area typically costs $1,100 to $1,200, booked about a week ahead. SUVs and pickups add $100 to $250, enclosed adds 30 to 50 percent, and summer demand can add $100. From Buffalo the price runs $100 to $150 lower because the drive is 300 miles shorter.

Why do Vail and Aspen cost so much more than Denver?

A full-size hauler is not practical over the Eisenhower Tunnel and Vail Pass, and the chain law applies September through May. Most mountain cars are transferred to a smaller truck in Denver, which adds $200 to $400 and one to three days. Picking the car up in Denver yourself avoids the surcharge entirely.

How long does New York to Colorado car shipping take?

Four to six days on the truck for Front Range destinations, after a pickup window of one to five days. Mountain towns add one to three days for the transfer. Winter blizzards that close I-80 across Nebraska can hold a carrier for a day, so build slack into a December or January delivery.

Do I need to remove my roof box or bike rack before shipping?

Yes. Roof boxes and racks can push a tall vehicle over the carrier's height limit, which triggers a surcharge or a refused load, and accessories are not covered by the carrier's cargo insurance. Take off the box, bike rack, and crossbars, and either ship them with your household goods or pack them in the trunk only if the driver agrees.

Will my New York car need an emissions test to register in Colorado?

In the Denver, Boulder, and northern Front Range counties, gasoline cars more than seven model years old need an Air Care Colorado emissions test, and any out-of-state car needs a VIN verification. Colorado Springs and the mountain counties have no emissions test. Newer cars are exempt from the test but still need the VIN check for a Colorado title.
