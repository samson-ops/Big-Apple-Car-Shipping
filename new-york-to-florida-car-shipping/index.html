New York to Florida car shipping is the single busiest lane in the United States, and that works in your favor. Carriers run I-95 from the NYC metro to South Florida constantly, which means more pickup dates, shorter waits, and tighter pricing than almost any other long-distance route. A running mid-size sedan on an open carrier typically costs $650 to $750 to the main Florida metros, with 2 to 4 days on the truck.

This guide covers what actually moves the price: season, destination, gated-community rules, hurricanes, and the student and snowbird spikes. All ten of our New York offices book this route, and the Bronx and Long Island offices have trucks loading for Florida daily. Get a live number from the quote tool beside this page.

The I-95 run: 1,100 to 1,300 miles in 2 to 4 days

From the five boroughs, Long Island, or Westchester, a carrier takes I-95 essentially the whole way: the New Jersey Turnpike, then Delaware, Maryland, Virginia, the Carolinas, and Georgia before entering Florida at Jacksonville.

A driver covering 500 to 600 miles a day reaches Orlando on the second or third day and Miami on the third or fourth. The pickup window is usually 1 to 3 days from your first available date, and same-week pickup is realistic most of the year. Trucks out of the Islip area often sell out their Florida slots before reaching New Jersey. Expedited car shipping on this route means a guaranteed pickup date rather than faster driving.

New York to Florida car shipping prices by season

Direction and month drive the price more than anything else. From December through February, demand to go south is heavy and demand to come north is thin. Trucks fill southbound and return half empty, so the southbound price carries a premium of roughly $100 to $200. Book two to three weeks out in that window.

From March through May the pattern flips. Seasonal residents head home, trucks fill northbound, and a car going south becomes the backhaul a carrier wants at a discount. This is the cheapest time to send a car south and the most expensive time to bring one back. June through August is busy both ways because of summer relocations. September through November, outside hurricane disruptions, is quiet and well priced in both directions.

On top of the season: SUVs and pickups add $100 to $250, an inoperable car adds $100 to $200, and enclosed runs 30 to 50 percent more. The car shipping cost page breaks those down.

New York to Florida car shipping by destination: South Florida to the Panhandle

Carriers do not treat Florida as one place. South Florida, meaning Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Boca Raton, and West Palm Beach, is the end of the line for most I-95 trucks and the best-served destination in the state. Delivery addresses there are often condo towers or gated streets, so expect to meet the driver at a nearby lot.

Orlando and Tampa get strong coverage because the I-4 corridor is a natural second stop for trucks that drop cars at Jacksonville and continue south. Naples and Fort Myers are further off the flow, reached via I-75 or Alligator Alley, and run $25 to $75 higher. Jacksonville is the first stop and often the cheapest. The Panhandle is the exception: Pensacola, Destin, Panama City, and Tallahassee sit on I-10, not I-95, and fewer trucks go there. Expect $100 to $150 more than the Miami rate and a longer pickup window.

Gated communities and HOA truck rules

A large share of Florida deliveries go into gated communities, 55-plus developments, and HOA neighborhoods, and many restrict commercial trucks. Some gates will not admit a 75-foot hauler; some prohibit unloading on the street; some require advance notice with the driver's name and plate.

Ask the gate office before the truck arrives, and if the answer is no, pick a meeting point such as a shopping center lot outside the gate. Tell us at booking so it goes on the dispatch sheet. The same applies on the New York side; a hauler cannot load on a narrow Brooklyn block. Our door-to-door car shipping page explains the handoff when the truck cannot reach the door.

Snowbirds and students: the two calendar spikes

Snowbirds head south from late October through January and return from March through early May. Book a December pickup three to four weeks ahead, and the April return equally early. The snowbird car shipping page covers round-trip booking.

Students are the second spike. UF, Florida State, UCF, the University of Miami, and FAU all pull New York families. The crunch is the second and third weeks of August for move-in and the first two weeks of May for move-out. Campus lots are closed to haulers, so plan to meet at an off-campus apartment or shopping center.

Hurricane season, June through November

Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, heaviest from mid-August through October. Carriers will not deliver into an evacuation zone, and they hold loads in Georgia or South Carolina until the state reopens. Cars already on trucks wait in a yard and arrive a few days late; cars not yet picked up get pushed back, sometimes a week.

If your move falls in that window, build in slack. The car shipping checklist lists the paperwork to keep handy if a delivery is rerouted.

Bringing a Florida car north: rust-free purchases

New Yorkers buy cars in Florida because they have never seen road salt, and a ten-year-old Florida car often has a cleaner underbody than a three-year-old one from Long Island. Dealers release a car to a carrier with a bill of sale and a signed release.

On arrival, an out-of-state vehicle needs a New York safety and emissions inspection within ten days of registration, and the DMV wants the title, bill of sale, proof of insurance, and form MV-82. A Florida-to-New York run in March or April carries the northbound premium; the same car in September ships at the base rate.

Questions about New York to Florida car shipping

How long does it take to ship a car from New York to Florida?

Plan on 1 to 3 days for pickup after your first available date and 2 to 4 days on the truck. Jacksonville and Orlando arrive on the second or third day; Miami and the Gulf coast on the third or fourth. Total door-to-door time is usually under a week outside the January and April peaks.

What does New York to Florida car shipping cost?

A running mid-size sedan on an open carrier is typically $650 to $750 to the major metros. Add $100 to $250 for an SUV or pickup, $100 to $200 if the car does not run, and 30 to 50 percent for enclosed. The Panhandle runs $100 to $150 more. Winter southbound and spring northbound carry a seasonal premium.

Can the truck deliver inside my gated community?

Often not. Many Florida HOAs and gated developments bar large commercial trucks or prohibit unloading on the street. Check with your gate office before pickup and give us a meeting point, usually a nearby shopping center lot. The driver will call ahead and meet you there to unload and complete the inspection.

What happens if a hurricane hits while my car is in transit?

The carrier holds the load in a safe area, usually Georgia or South Carolina, until roads and the delivery zone reopen. Your car stays on the truck or in a secured yard and is covered by the carrier's cargo insurance throughout. Expect a delay of a few days to a week. Cars not yet picked up are rescheduled.

What paperwork do I need to bring a Florida car to New York?

For pickup, the carrier needs the keys and a release from the seller or dealer. For registration, bring the title, bill of sale, proof of New York insurance, and form MV-82 to the DMV, then pass a New York safety and emissions inspection within ten days. Sales tax is paid at registration if not collected in Florida.
